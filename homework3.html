Experiment

A Bernoulli trial has two possible outcomes: success (1) and failure (0). The probability of success is p. As the number of trials increases, the cumulative sample proportion should tend to become closer to the theoretical probability.

Discussion

For a Bernoulli random variable, the expected value is E[X] = p. The sample mean is the same as the proportion of successes because each observation is either 0 or 1.

The Law of Large Numbers states that the sample mean converges to the expected value as the number of independent trials increases. The convergence is not necessarily monotonic: the observed proportion can move both towards and away from p during a finite simulation.

Repeating the simulation can produce different paths, but longer runs generally show a smaller and more stable difference between the observed proportion and p.
