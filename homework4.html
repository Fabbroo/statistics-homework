Homework 4 - Welford's Recurrence Formulas

Online estimation of sampling means and unbiased sampling variances

Back to Home

What the experiment studies

Each of m hackers attempts to penetrate n systems. Every attempt receives an integer score from 0 to 10. For each hacker, the sample mean and sample variance are computed online with Welford's recurrence, without storing the complete trajectory.

The experiment is repeated for n = 20, 100, and 1000. Across all hackers, we compare the average sample mean with the theoretical mean μ, and the average unbiased sample variance with the theoretical variance σ².

Theory

1. Chosen score distribution

The score X is discrete uniform on {0,1,...,10}:

P(X = x) = 1/11,   x = 0,1,...,10.

Therefore:

μ = E[X] = 5
σ² = Var(X) = 10

2. Mean recurrence

After observing xn, define δ = xn - Mn-1. Then:

Mn = Mn-1 + δ/n

This is Knuth's online recurrence for the arithmetic mean.

3. Sum-of-squares recurrence

Let M2n be the sum of squared deviations from the current mean:

M2n = Σi=1n(xi - Mn)²

Welford's recurrence is:

M2n = M2n-1 + (xn - Mn-1)(xn - Mn)

4. Variance

Population-form variance: M2n/n

Unbiased sample variance: s² = M2n/(n-1)

This homework uses the unbiased sample variance because its expected value is σ² when the observations are IID and n > 1.

Core Welford update

const delta = x - mean;
mean += delta / count;
M2 += delta * (x - mean);

Why the M2 recurrence is true

Start from M2n = Σ(xi - Mn)². For the first n-1 observations, write xi - Mn as (xi - Mn-1) + (Mn-1 - Mn). When the squares are expanded, the cross term vanishes because Σi=1n-1(xi - Mn-1) = 0. Substituting Mn - Mn-1 = δ/n and simplifying gives M2n = M2n-1 + δ(xn - Mn).

Conclusions and inferential interpretation

Average of the sample means

It should be close to μ = 5 because the sample mean is unbiased: E[X̄] = μ. Increasing n does not change its center, but reduces its variance to σ²/n = 10/n.

Average of the sample variances

It should be close to σ² = 10 because division by n-1 makes s² an unbiased estimator. Individual estimates vary, but their spread decreases as n increases.

Why this matters

Inferential statistics uses sample statistics to estimate unknown population parameters. Larger samples produce more precise estimates, which leads to smaller standard errors and narrower confidence intervals.
